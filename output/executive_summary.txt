# Customer Analytics — Executive Summary

**Project:** Customer Segmentation, Retention & Revenue Analysis
**Author:** Wei
**Tools:** Python (pandas, matplotlib, seaborn), MySQL, Power BI
**Dataset:** UCI Online Retail Dataset

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes e-commerce transaction data to understand customer value, purchasing behavior, retention performance, and churn risk.

The objective is to transform raw transaction data into actionable business insights through SQL analysis, Python data analytics, RFM segmentation, cohort retention analysis, and customer risk identification.

## 2. SQL Business Overview

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|

| Total sales | £10,642,110.80 |
| Average order value | £533.17 |

## 3. RFM Customer Segmentation

The RFM analysis identified 4,338 customers and classified them into seven customer segments.

| Customer Segment | Customers | Revenue Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Core High-Value Customers | 925 | 67.12% |
| High-Potential Customers | 395 | 9.96% |
| Historical High-Spend Dormant | 243 | 6.97% |
| Active Repeat Customers | 500 | 6.09% |
| General Dormant Customers | 1,206 | 4.65% |
| Recent Low-Frequency Customers | 759 | 3.16% |
| General Maintenance Customers | 310 | 2.05% |

**Key Finding:**

Core high-value customers represent approximately 21.32% of the customer base but contribute 67.12% of RFM-based customer spending.

Customer retention and loyalty among this segment should be a major business priority.

Total RFM customer spending: £8,887,208.89.

## 4. Cohort Retention Analysis

Customers were grouped by their first recorded purchase month to evaluate repeat purchasing behavior over time.

**Updated Key Findings:**

- December 2010 cohort: 36.61% Month-1 retention.
- January 2011 cohort: 22.06% Month-1 retention.
- February 2011 cohort: 18.68% Month-1 retention.
- March 2011 cohort: 15.04% Month-1 retention.
- April 2011 cohort: 21.33% Month-1 retention.

Several cohorts experienced substantial decreases in purchasing activity after their first purchase month.

The results suggest an opportunity to improve early customer retention through second-purchase incentives, personalized recommendations, and targeted follow-up communication.

**Limitation:** The dataset ends on December 9, 2011. Retention results involving December 2011 are affected by incomplete observation periods and should be interpreted cautiously.

## 5. Churn Risk Analysis

Customers with historical purchasing activity and extended periods of inactivity were identified for potential re-engagement.

**At-Risk Customer Results:**

| Risk Segment | Customers |
|---|---:|
| At Risk | 235 |
| Needs Attention | 72 |
| Cannot Lose | 4 |
| **Total** | **311** |

These exploratory risk labels use a different framework from the final seven RFM segments; they must be validated before operational use.

## 6. Key Business Recommendations

**1. Protect High-Value Customers**

Develop loyalty programs, exclusive offers, and personalized engagement strategies for core high-value customers.

**2. Improve First-Month Retention**

Introduce post-purchase reminders, targeted promotions, and second-purchase incentives to encourage repeat transactions.

**3. Launch Win-Back Campaigns**

Prioritize inactive customers with high historical spending and develop personalized re-engagement campaigns.

**4. Investigate Cohort Performance**

Investigate possible explanations for the December 2010 cohort’s higher retention, taking cohort differences and incomplete observation windows into account.

**5. Develop Customer-Specific Marketing**

Use RFM segments to design differentiated marketing strategies rather than applying the same campaign to every customer.

## 7. Conclusion

This project demonstrates how SQL, Python, and customer analytics techniques can transform transaction-level data into practical business insights.

The analysis highlights customer spending concentration, retention, purchasing frequency, and exploratory churn-risk indicators. It does not estimate predictive customer lifetime value.

The main business opportunity is to protect high-value customers, improve repeat purchasing, and identify customers who require targeted retention efforts.
