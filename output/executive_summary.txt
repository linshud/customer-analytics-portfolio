# Customer Analytics — Executive Summary

**Project:** Customer Segmentation, Retention & Revenue Analysis  
**Author:** Wei  
**Tools:** Python (pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn), MySQL 8, Power BI, Excel  
**Dataset:** UCI Online Retail Dataset  
**Analysis period:** December 1, 2010 – December 9, 2011

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes historical e-commerce transactions to understand customer value, purchasing behavior, repeat purchasing, and re-engagement opportunities. The workflow combines Python data preparation and RFM segmentation, MySQL sales analysis, cohort retention analysis, and a Power BI dashboard.

The transaction-level sales analysis includes valid positive-price sales lines. Customer-level RFM and cohort analyses use only transactions with an identifiable CustomerID.

## 3. RFM Customer Segmentation

RFM summarizes customer behavior using Recency (days since last purchase), Frequency (distinct invoices), and Monetary (identified-customer spending). Each measure is scored from 1 to 5, and the resulting 4,338 customers are assigned to seven business-oriented segments.

| Customer segment | Customers | Share of identified-customer spending |
|---|---:|---:|
| Core High-Value Customers | 925 | 67.12% |
| High-Potential Customers | 395 | 9.96% |
| Historical High-Spend Dormant Customers | 243 | 6.97% |
| Active Repeat Customers | 500 | 6.09% |
| General Dormant Customers | 1,206 | 4.65% |
| Recent Low-Frequency Customers | 759 | 3.16% |
| General Maintenance Customers | 310 | 2.05% |
| **Total** | **4,338** | **100.00%** |

**Identified-customer spending:** £8,887,208.89.

**Key finding:** The 925 core high-value customers represent approximately **21.32% of identifiable customers** but contribute **67.12% of identified-customer spending**. Retaining this group is a high-priority opportunity.

## 4. Cohort Retention Analysis

Customers are grouped by their first recorded purchase month. Month-1 retention is the percentage of a cohort that purchased again in the following calendar month.

| First-purchase cohort | Month-1 retention |
|---|---:|
| December 2010 | 36.61% |
| January 2011 | 22.06% |
| February 2011 | 18.68% |
| March 2011 | 15.04% |
| April 2011 | 21.33% |
| May 2011 | 19.01% |
| November 2011 | 11.15% |

Several cohorts have Month-1 retention below 25%, suggesting an opportunity to improve early repeat purchasing through relevant follow-ups and second-purchase engagement.

**Observation-window limitation:** The dataset ends on December 9, 2011. December 2011 is only partially observed, and blank cells in the cohort matrix represent months outside the observation window, not zero retention.

## 6. Re-engagement Opportunity Exploration

The updated exploratory chart highlights three of the seven RFM segments for closer retention and win-back attention:

| Selected RFM segment | Customers |
|---|---:|
| General Dormant Customers | 1,206 |
| Recent Low-Frequency Customers | 759 |
| Historical High-Spend Dormant Customers | 243 |
| **Total shown** | **2,208** |

These are existing RFM segments, not separate churn-model classes. The chart is a descriptive view of historical purchase behavior; it is **not a trained or validated churn-prediction model**. Historical High-Spend Dormant Customers may be candidates for win-back outreach, while Recent Low-Frequency Customers may warrant second-purchase engagement. Campaign eligibility and rules should be validated before operational use.

## 7. Key Business Recommendations

1. **Protect core high-value customers.** Test personalized loyalty benefits and proactive service for customers contributing the largest share of identified-customer spending.
2. **Encourage a second purchase.** Test post-purchase follow-ups, relevant recommendations, and targeted incentives during the first month after acquisition.
3. **Re-engage formerly valuable customers.** Prioritize dormant high-spend customers for carefully measured win-back campaigns.
4. **Improve CustomerID capture.** Investigate the 132,186 unidentified sales lines to improve future customer-level analysis coverage.
5. **Monitor comparable cohorts.** Use consistent observation windows and treat partial-month observations separately.

These are recommendations from descriptive analysis. Their effects have not been established by controlled experiments.

## 8. Limitations and Project Status

- The dataset is historical and does not represent current business performance.
- Transactions without a CustomerID cannot be attributed to an identified customer.
- December 2011 is an incomplete observation month.
- RFM summarizes historical behavior; it does not directly measure future customer lifetime value.
- The re-engagement chart highlights three RFM segments and is not a validated churn model.
- Full reproduction requires the original transaction dataset and local database / Power BI configuration, which are not included in this lightweight portfolio repository.

**Project status:** Core analytical work completed. Selected derived CSV outputs, visualizations, SQL scripts, documentation, and the Power BI report are included in the repository.
